# Investment philosophy

I want the strongest names from the past year that beat my benchmark — stocks
and ETFs most likely to *keep* doing well. But the first job is downside
protection: minimize the chance anything I buy goes negative right after I buy
it. I'd rather miss gains than own a blowup. No system can eliminate losses;
this one should make them less likely, not pretend they're impossible.

**How that turns into rules:**

- Every pick must earn its place. A screen full of slots is worthless if the
  bottom half is filler — quality over quantity, always. An honest short list
  beats a padded long one.
- Estimates are expected values, not forecasts. A great past year is evidence,
  not a promise; the method should assume hot streaks cool and thin evidence
  means humility, not confidence.
- No event-driven names. If the run depends on a war, a headline, or a binary
  catalyst, it's suspect. I want durable compounding, not trades wearing a
  disguise.
- American names only — US-domiciled stocks, US-focused ETFs. And diversified
  by construction: no accidental concentration in one sector, theme, or story.
- No religious-themed securities. No faith-based screens, religious mandates,
  or religiously-branded issuers in any holding — stocks, ETFs, or otherwise.
- The process must be auditable. Any good analyst reading the record of a run
  should be able to check the work, disagree with a stage, and propose a
  better one. If the method can't be questioned, it can't be trusted.
