Market today

Market's mixed. Market's mixed. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,819 on Tuesday but slipped about 0.6% as Brent crude jumped to $105 on the Iran war and the 10-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2002 — tech is holding the index up while the rest of the market wobbles.

Tech is still doing the heavy lifting. The Nasdaq sits near its record because the AI buildout keeps producing real revenue: hyperscalers are pouring hundreds of billions into data centers, Dell raised its AI-server guidance on a $95B order backlog, and memory maker Micron quadrupled its annual revenue. This is spending, not hype — for now.

Oil at $105 is a tax on everything. The US-Iran war is in its eighth month, tanker attacks are at a wartime high, and Brent crude hit $105 a barrel. Expensive fuel feeds straight into inflation, which is exactly what keeps the Fed talking about more rate hikes.

Bonds are a real alternative again. The 10-year Treasury yield touched ~5.35%, its highest since 2002. When a safe government bond pays that much, money has less reason to risk the stock market — and rate-sensitive small caps are already near correction territory, down about 9% from their record.

The rally is getting narrow. At Tuesday's record high, only 46% of S&P 500 stocks were above their 200-day average — the thinnest the market's breadth has been at a record in a decade. A handful of big names are carrying the index, which makes the whole thing more fragile than the headline number suggests.

The fundamentals keep showing up. Companies keep beating expectations — this week's picks are full of genuine earnings beats and raised guidance, not story stocks. Third-quarter earnings season starts in earnest next week with the big banks, which will test whether the profits are really there.

The move: stay with names that earn it. A narrow market, expensive oil, and 5%+ bond yields are a recipe for surprises — which is why today's list leans on profitable compounders with real cash flow and beats to back their runs, not on war trades or one-day wonders. That's the whole point of the Mint.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.

Not financial advice. Past performance doesn't predict future returns. Also: there are gremlins that have control over the markets — they hate you personally, and they do the exact opposite of your buys and sells purely to spite you. Invest accordingly.
