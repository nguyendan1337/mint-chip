Market today

Market's up. The market is advancing again after its shaky October stretch. The S&P 500 has risen four straight sessions to 7,743.98 and the Nasdaq to 26,549.85, led by chip and AI names, and the rebound has now survived the Federal Reserve's decision to hold rates steady.

Chips and AI are leading the rebound South Korea's SK Hynix jumped 10.9% on bumper memory-chip results, saying AI demand will outpace supply through 2030, and that lifted Micron 5.9% and the whole chip complex. When the companies selling the picks and shovels of AI report this kind of demand, the rest of tech tends to follow.

Earnings are doing the heavy lifting This week's prints keep beating expectations and raising guidance, from IDEX's record quarter to PC Connection's sales-led beat and Hasbro's Magic-driven surge. Companies are proving they can grow profits even with borrowing costs high, which is exactly what a rally needs to last.

Oil and the Hormuz war keep the inflation squeeze on October has seen a record 28 attacks on ships around the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran formally declared the waterway closed on October 26. When oil shipments are threatened, fuel prices rise, inflation stays hot, and the Fed stays hawkish, which is why safe bonds paying around 5% are still competing with stocks for investors' money.

A packed week of tests is still ahead Thursday brings the first estimate of third-quarter economic growth plus September inflation data, next Tuesday is the midterm election, and the October jobs report lands November 6. Any one of those could cool the rally, and big earnings from Fortinet and Twilio on October 28-29 will test whether tech's bounce is real.

The move: ride the earnings winners, respect the rate ceiling Today's 15 picks lean on companies already proving their growth with real numbers, not just AI stories, and five ETF slots stay empty because nothing else cleared the bar. That selectivity is the point: with rates near 5% and oil unsettled, only names earning their place make the list.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
