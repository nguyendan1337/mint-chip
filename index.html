Market today

Market's down. The broad market has been sliding — the S&P 500 gave up over 2% this week and the Nasdaq fell 2.6% on Thursday alone, as investors turned on Big Tech's AI spending while oil's spike to $117 per barrel stoked inflation fears.

Energy is carrying the market Oil jumped to about $117 a barrel, the highest since spring, after attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and renewed US-Iran escalation. Expensive oil is an inflation risk for everyone — but it is a profit windfall for producers, which is why energy names are leading while the rest of the market struggles.

Cybersecurity is having its moment A US government warning about a widespread software flaw — touching a huge share of websites — sent CrowdStrike up over 8% this week. AI is making software bugs faster to find and exploit, which is turning cybersecurity from a budget line into a board-level worry. That's why the sector ETF is the top-rated ETF pick today.

The AI-spending bill is coming due Meta beat earnings and still fell 7% because investors fixated on its projected $145 billion in 2026 AI spending. The market is now grading Big Tech on the cash it burns — data centers, chips, electricity — not just the revenue it grows. Semiconductors fell nearly 7% on Thursday as the AI-infrastructure trade unwound.

Safe bonds now pay ~5% The 10-year Treasury yield is sitting at 4.99%, and the Fed decides next week whether to raise rates again — a decision that stands for six weeks since there's no November meeting. When plain bonds pay that much, they pull money out of stocks, and growth stocks feel it most.

Money is leaving the winners October's pattern has been rotation out of mega-cap winners into small caps, cyclicals and energy — a textbook change in market leadership. Momentum-style funds that own recent winners are the casualty; value and quality factors are holding up, which is why the ETF picks lean that way.

The move: patience and cash flow This is a market punishing expensive promises and rewarding cash in hand. Picks are concentrated in profitable, conservatively-run companies — quality balance sheets and free cash flow over story stocks — because in a rate scare, the businesses that fund themselves are the ones that keep compounding.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
