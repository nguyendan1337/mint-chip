Market today

Market's mixed. The broad market is holding up while its biggest tech name is in crisis, with value and defensive stocks advancing and bond yields above 5 percent setting the ceiling.

Cheap and defensive stocks are carrying the market While Nvidia lost its chief executive and fell hard, the big indexes slipped only about 0.4 percent on Thursday because money rotated into value, health care and everyday goods. That broadening is what a healthier market looks like.

Today's inflation report gave some relief Producer prices rose 0.3 percent in October, less than the 0.4 percent expected, after a hot consumer price report earlier in the week. It is the first inflation number in days that does not argue for a bigger rate hike.

Nvidia's leadership crisis hangs over tech Jensen Huang resigned after a board investigation, the finance chief is running the company for now, and Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday. Because Nvidia is one of the largest weights in the indexes, its next move moves everyone's savings.

Safe bonds now pay over 5 percent The 10 year Treasury yield sits near 5.1 percent and traders treat a December rate hike as nearly certain. Safe bonds paying that much compete directly with stocks for money, which caps how high stock prices can go.

War keeps an oil premium in prices Attacks on tankers and Saudi energy sites around the Strait of Hormuz settled oil near 96 dollars on Thursday. Expensive oil feeds inflation, and inflation is exactly what is forcing the central bank toward another hike.

The bottom line This is a market that rewards owning profitable, reasonably priced companies and punishes betting everything on one story. Today's picks lean that way on purpose: drug distributors, medical supplies, water equipment and broad cheap stock funds over high flying tech.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected return for a stock: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read. A fund carries the same idea under its own label, the ETF score: a standing score from 0 to 100 that ranks the fund against the other researched funds on cost, size and short-term price strength — not a forecast for that one fund.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
