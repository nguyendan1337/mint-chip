Market today

Market's mixed. Stocks are near their highs after a strong earnings season, but the market is split: the broad market held up after the Fed stood pat, while big tech wobbled on Alphabet's earnings miss and the 10-year Treasury yield near 5% keeps competing with stocks for investors' money.

Earnings are doing the heavy lifting About 84% of S&P 500 companies reporting so far have beaten expectations, with third-quarter earnings on track to rise roughly 32% from a year ago. That profit strength is why the market is holding near its highs despite everything else going on.

The Fed stood pat, and banks and refiners led The Federal Reserve held its key rate at 3.75% to 4% on October 28, and stocks rose that day, with banks gaining. Energy and refining names are also strong, helped by high oil prices that fatten refining margins.

Oil near $114 is an inflation tax on everyone else Brent crude trades near $114 a barrel because attacks on tankers and Saudi infrastructure keep a war premium in oil. Expensive oil pushes up gas and shipping costs, which feeds inflation and keeps interest rates high — the main reason the Fed is in no hurry to cut.

Big tech's AI spending is now being graded, not cheered Alphabet fell more than 10% after missing expectations and reporting negative free cash flow, dragging the Nasdaq down. Microsoft rose on strong cloud growth, so the market is now rewarding AI spending that already pays and punishing spending that doesn't — a much pickier mood than earlier this year.

A busy, high-stakes week is coming The midterm elections land November 3, the jobs report follows November 6, and October inflation data arrives November 10. A hot inflation reading would lock in a December rate hike that markets already mostly expect, and push bond yields higher still.

The move: stay with profitable compounders, not stories With bond yields near 5% and oil near $114, money has good alternatives to stocks, so only companies actually converting growth into cash keep earning their place. That is exactly what today's picks lean on: record earnings, real cash flow, and raised guidance — not promises.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
