Market today

Market's mixed. The broad market has stalled after two down weeks, with tech lagging while smaller stocks hold up better. The S&P 500 slipped about 0.4% last week to close near $760, right on support it has held since early October, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq fell harder on a sharp selloff in chip and AI names; tech fund VGT is down about 4% over the past month. Today's bounce does not yet change that picture.

Smaller stocks and defensive corners are holding up While big tech wobbled, small caps gained about 2% last week and steady dividend and value names kept attracting money. That rotation, not an exodus, is why the broad market is flat rather than falling: investors are moving toward steadier businesses instead of selling stocks altogether.

Earnings are doing the heavy lifting for today's picks Several of today's top names got here on real results, not hype: SharkNinja raised its full-year outlook after a 13th straight quarter of double-digit sales growth, and Concentra and West Pharmaceutical both beat expectations and raised guidance. When companies deliver, the market still rewards them.

Oil near $117 and yields near 5% are squeezing everyone else Attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz have kept crude near six-month highs, which pushes up gas prices and keeps inflation warm. At the same time, safe government bonds pay close to 5%, which gives investors a real alternative to stocks and makes expensive growth names harder to justify.

A huge week of tests is coming The Federal Reserve announces its rate decision Wednesday, with markets expecting it to hold rates steady, and Thursday brings fresh inflation and growth numbers. Big tech reports too, days after one disappointing AI-spending update triggered the Nasdaq's worst day since June. Any surprise on rates, inflation, or tech earnings could move the market quickly.

The move: stay selective, not fearful The market is not breaking down, but it is not rewarding everything either. The names still earning a place are the ones with durable businesses and fresh earnings momentum, while event-driven stories and stretched valuations are being left behind. That is exactly the environment this list is built for.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
