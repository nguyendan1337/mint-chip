Market chat

The market is up — the Nasdaq hit a record Friday after a weak jobs report made investors bet the Fed will leave rates alone, keeping borrowing cheap, while AI spending keeps powering big tech. The shadow over the rally is the seven-month US-Iran war: fighting near the Strait of Hormuz keeps oil around $100, raising costs for everyone and feeding inflation — just as borrowing costs sit at 24-year highs.

As of 2026-10-03.

Technology

Technology is where the market's energy is. AI spending is driving real revenue — chips, cloud, and cybersecurity all see strong demand as companies race to build out AI. The risk is concentration: so much money is crowded into the same AI trade that any disappointment could sting.

CIBR First Trust NASDAQ Cybersecurity ETF ETF
An ETF holding US-listed cybersecurity companies.
Global security spending is expected to grow about 13% this year to roughly $245 billion, with AI-related security spending doubling — this fund rides that structural demand directly.
FTNT Fortinet, Inc. STOCK
Fortinet sells cybersecurity software and hardware that protects corporate networks.
Revenue grew 26% last quarter, billings jumped 33%, and it raised its full-year forecast. As AI expands what hackers can attack, companies keep spending on security — steady demand, not a one-time event.
ARW Arrow Electronics, Inc. STOCK
Arrow Electronics distributes electronic components and tech hardware to manufacturers around the world.
It just won full distribution rights for HPE's networking gear and expanded its IBM partnership across Europe, capping three straight quarters of triple-digit earnings growth.
SKYY First Trust Cloud Computing ETF ETF
An ETF holding cloud computing companies.
Public cloud spending is on track to pass $1 trillion this year, up 21%. The wrinkle: AI is disrupting some software business models even as it boosts cloud infrastructure, and this fund holds both sides.
Financial Services

Asset managers are having a great year. Rising markets lift the assets they manage — and the fees they earn on them — and both of these firms are also pulling in fresh client money quarter after quarter.

AAMI Acadian Asset Management Inc. STOCK
Acadian is an asset management firm running systematic, data-driven investment portfolios for institutions.
Last quarter was a record: earnings beat estimates by 28%, revenue up 45%, assets at a record $233 billion with ten straight quarters of inflows. It has also shrunk its share count 58% since 2019, boosting each remaining share.
VCTR Victory Capital Holdings, Inc. STOCK
Victory Capital is an asset manager offering specialized investment strategies to institutions and retirement platforms.
A record quarter too — $346 billion in client assets, $4.2 billion of net inflows, and over 70% of its funds beating their benchmarks. Growth is coming from several directions, not one lucky bet.
Industrials

Industrial companies are benefiting from steady business investment. These two make the unglamorous equipment other industries run on, and both are posting solid growth with raised forecasts.

IEX IDEX Corporation STOCK
IDEX makes pumps, valves, and other engineered equipment for industrial and scientific uses.
Beat earnings in July, raised its full-year forecast, and just lifted its dividend for the 16th straight year. Analysts have been raising price targets into October.
NDSN Nordson Corporation STOCK
Nordson makes precision equipment that dispenses adhesives, coatings, and fluids for manufacturers.
Fiscal third-quarter sales and earnings both beat, full-year guidance raised, order backlog up 35%, and the dividend just went up. Demand looks broad-based, not tied to one customer or event.
Real Estate

Hotel owners are riding a strong travel stretch. Room revenues are up double digits, convention season looks strong, and both of these landlords are sharing the gains with shareholders.

APLE Apple Hospitality REIT, Inc. STOCK
Apple Hospitality owns 216 upscale hotels across the US.
Declared a fresh monthly dividend of about 6.2% annualized, backed by an earnings beat, and analysts at several banks raised their targets. Industry data shows US hotel revenues up 14% from last year.
PK Park Hotels & Resorts Inc. STOCK
Park Hotels owns 30 premium hotels and resorts, one of the largest lodging REITs.
Raised its room-revenue forecast, was upgraded to Strong Buy in late September, and just repaid a $1.3 billion loan — cleaning up the balance sheet while travel demand stays strong.
Healthcare

Two very different healthcare stories: one steady compounder in workplace health, one generic-drug maker with a fresh product approval.

CON Concentra Group Holdings Parent, Inc. STOCK
Concentra runs occupational health clinics — workplace injury care for employers across the US.
Four straight earnings beats and a stack of fresh analyst upgrades. Demand for workplace health is steady in almost any economy.
AMRX Amneal Pharmaceuticals, Inc. STOCK
Amneal makes generic and specialty drugs.
Won FDA approval in September for a generic version of a nearly $1 billion drug and launched it immediately. The underlying business is improving too — last quarter's earnings beat estimates.
Mid-Cap Value

One fund, one idea: own profitable American companies that generate lots of cash.

VFLO Victoryshares Free Cash Flow ETF ETF
An ETF that picks 50 profitable US large caps with strong free cash flow.
Up 38% over the past year on a durable trend — investors keep rewarding companies that actually generate cash. Rules-based and diversified, so no single stock can sink it.
Large Growth

Pure mega-cap tech exposure — the biggest Nasdaq names in one basket.

QTOP iShares Nasdaq Top 30 Stocks ETF ETF
An ETF holding the 30 largest Nasdaq stocks — Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia and friends.
Up 28% over the past year tracking the AI and mega-cap rally. The tradeoff is concentration: a handful of giants drive most of the return, so it falls hard if sentiment turns.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.

Not financial advice. Past performance doesn't predict future returns. Also: there are gremlins that have control over the markets, they hate you personally, and they will do the exact opposite of your buys and sells out of pure spite.
