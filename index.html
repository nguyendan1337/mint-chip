Market today

Market's mixed. Tech and AI names slid on debt fears and hot inflation data — the Nasdaq had its worst week since June — while energy and defensives held firm; Monday is opening green as chip stocks rebound.

Inflation hasn't stopped the earners. Travelers beat big Friday — $9.18 a share vs. $7.39 expected — and banks, insurers, and asset managers keep posting clean quarters. Roughly 70% of S&P members rose over the last two weeks even as the index fell: money is finding companies that deliver, not just promise.

Hot inflation is repricing the Fed. September CPI hit 3.7% and wholesale inflation 6.6%, a four-year high. The 10-year Treasury now pays 5.1% — and when safe bonds pay that much, they compete directly with stocks. The Oct 28 Fed meeting is next, and the next move looks more likely up than down.

The AI buildout is splitting in two. The Nasdaq had its worst week since June (-4.5%) as investors questioned debt-funded data-center spending, and semiconductors fell seven straight sessions. Chips are rebounding Monday (Sandisk +7.4%, Micron +4.7%) — this is fear about how the boom is financed, not whether AI needs chips.

Oil is giving energy a second wind. Crude sits near $100 after new attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz and fresh sanctions on Russian energy. Disrupted supply means higher fuel prices, which feed inflation and keep rate pressure on — and energy was Friday's best sector (+2.5%) while tech fell 2.5%.

The move: own earners, not stories. Monday's board leans where the market is paying: asset managers compounding client flows, insurers beating on underwriting, cash-flow ETFs built for a 5.1% world. This week's earnings — Netflix, Meta, Tesla — decide whether tech's dip is a buy or a warning.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
