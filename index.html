Market today

Market's mixed. Tech is sliding while the rest of the market holds up: the Nasdaq just posted its worst month since March 2025 on doubts about AI spending, but the Dow actually gained in October as money rotated into steadier, dividend-paying names.

Steady earners are carrying the market while tech wobbles. With big tech stumbling, investors are paying up for companies with real cash flow and dividends instead of just growth stories. That rotation is why this week's picks lean toward asset managers, insurers, health care and energy rather than the high-flying tech names of earlier this year.

Big tech's AI spending spree is facing its first real skepticism. Alphabet missed expectations and posted negative free cash flow, Amazon missed while planning about $220 billion of AI data-center spending, and Intel plunged roughly 29% on weak guidance. When investors start doubting the payoff from AI spending, they sell the chip and mega-cap names that drove the market, even though 82% of S&P 500 companies reporting so far beat earnings.

Oil near $116 is a tax on everything else. Attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway most Gulf oil ships through, plus talk of renewed US strikes on Iran after next week's midterm elections, pushed Brent crude to about $116 a barrel. Higher oil means higher fuel and shipping costs, which feeds into inflation and makes it harder for the Federal Reserve to cut interest rates.

Most companies are still beating expectations. Look past the tech headlines and earnings season is actually healthy: more than four in five S&P 500 companies that have reported third-quarter results beat forecasts. The selloff is about what investors are willing to pay for growth, not a collapse in profits.

The move: favor cash flow over stories until the AI-spending doubt clears. With the 10-year Treasury bond paying nearly 4.93%, safe bonds now compete with stocks for your money, so the market is rewarding businesses that already generate cash. Next week's jobs report on November 6 and inflation report on November 10 will say whether that caution lifts or deepens.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.

Not financial advice. Past performance doesn't predict future returns. Also: there are gremlins that have control over the markets — they hate you personally, and they do the exact opposite of your buys and sells purely to spite you. Invest accordingly.
