Market's mixed. The market is bouncing back from its worst week since April 2025, but the rebound is uneven: almost everything is up except the biggest AI name, and Tuesday's inflation report will decide whether the bounce lasts.

The bounce is broadening Stocks opened sharply higher on Monday, with the S&P 500 up about 1.7% by mid morning after its worst week since April 2025. The gains spread into banks, airlines and healthcare instead of riding on one or two tech giants, which is a healthier kind of rally.

The market rose anyway, so the AI leader is splitting from the rest of the market rather than dragging it down.

The Fed may hike in December October jobs beat expectations, with 165,000 added against about 65,000 expected, and traders now put roughly 80% odds on a rate hike at the Fed's December meeting. A hot inflation report on Tuesday would push those odds higher, and higher rates make safe bonds pay enough to compete with stocks.

Oil is getting cheaper Brent crude fell to about $95 a barrel, down roughly 15% on the week, as traders priced hopes of a deal to end the US-Iran war. Cheaper oil takes pressure off inflation and interest rates, which helps stocks. But attacks in the Strait of Hormuz are still happening and a deal is rated unlikely, so this could reverse fast.

A bruised market finding its footing The market took its worst hit since April, steadied on Friday, and is bouncing, but the test comes Tuesday with the inflation report. About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
