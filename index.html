Market today

Market's mixed. Market's mixed. The broad market held up this week — the S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,712.57 and the Dow rose 1% — but tech kept slipping as chip stocks fell for an eighth straight day, the longest streak since 2008.

Earnings season is doing the heavy lifting. Strong third-quarter results from Equifax, Hasbro and 3M lifted the Dow 1% and the S&P on Tuesday, and winners outside big tech jumped up to 11%. SpaceX posted $14.6B in quarterly revenue (+161%) and Super Micro raised its guidance on server demand, rallying semiconductors. Profits are what stocks are ultimately priced on, and profits are delivering.

The rotation is broadening, not just fading tech. Small caps jumped 1.65% on Tuesday while the Nasdaq sat flat — money is finding new leaders in value, quality and cash-flow names rather than just leaving. That broadening is exactly what today's value and quality-factor picks are positioned for.

The Iran war keeps oil near the danger zone. A record 12 Iranian attacks on ships around the Strait of Hormuz last week pushed Brent crude above $109 a barrel, and diesel hit a record $6.28 a gallon. When oil spikes, it feeds inflation, which keeps borrowing costs high — and high borrowing costs lower what investors will pay for stocks.

Safe bonds now pay 5%+, real competition for stocks. The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 5.15%, meaning investors can earn strong returns without touching the stock market at all. That pulls money out of equities and punishes anything without real earnings — September housing starts fell 5.2% as high rates bite.

The bottom line: a choppy climb, not a clean breakout. Strong profits and a broadening rally are the engine; $105 oil and 5% yields are the speed limit. With the Fed deciding Oct 28, war headlines swirling before the Nov 3 midterms, and big tech earnings this week, expect the market to keep earning its gains the hard way.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
