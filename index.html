Market today

Market's up. Market's up. The S&P 500 is up 14% this year and sitting at record highs, but the rally is narrow — more than 4 in 10 S&P stocks are actually down, with a handful of AI chipmakers doing most of the lifting.

The AI trade is still paying. Memory-chip makers like Sandisk and Micron have more than tripled this year on real AI demand — Dell just raised its revenue guidance to $192 billion on $60.9 billion of AI server orders. Earnings season opens Tuesday with the big banks, and that's the next test of whether the money spreads beyond chips.

Energy producers are swimming in it. Oil is near $105 a barrel with Middle East shipping under threat, and refiners and producers are earning record margins. Diesel hit record prices, which pads their bottom lines even as it taxes everyone else.

The Fed is hiking into a slowdown. The Fed raised rates in September and most officials think another hike is coming by year-end. The 10-year Treasury pays over 5% — its highest since 2002 — so safe bonds now compete with stocks, and heavily indebted companies pay more to borrow.

The war is in your gas tank. The US-Iran war keeps threatening Middle East oil shipments, and Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia pushed oil above $105 this weekend. Gasoline stays over $4 a gallon and diesel over $6, which is why consumer confidence keeps falling.

Wednesday's inflation report sets the tone. September CPI lands Wednesday morning — the last big inflation reading before the Fed's late-October meeting. A hot number makes another rate hike more likely and rattles rate-sensitive stocks; a cool one gives the rally breathing room.

The move: stay with earnings, watch Wednesday. The market is up, but it's a handful of AI and energy names carrying everything — 4 in 10 stocks are down on the year. Mint's picks lean into companies with real earnings growth and low dependence on headlines, which is the right posture while Wednesday's CPI and the Fed decide what comes next.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
