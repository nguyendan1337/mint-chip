Market today

Market's mixed. The market has whipsawed over the last few weeks: a sharp post election slide, then a strong rebound this week that leaves the S&P 500 within about 3% of its record, while hot inflation and a 10 year Treasury yield near 5% keep the recovery on a short leash.

Tech roared back today A strong earnings report from CoreWeave revived the AI trade: the S&P 500 rose about 1.3% and the Nasdaq about 1.6%, with Nvidia recovering after two down days. Third quarter earnings growth is running near 31%, so company profits, not just hope, are doing part of the lifting.

Cheaper oil is taking pressure off Brent crude has fallen about 19% since late October, to around $92, on hopes the Strait of Hormuz may reopen, though the report behind that hope is disputed. Cheaper oil means cheaper fuel and freight, which cools inflation and leaves households more to spend elsewhere.

Inflation came in hot, and a December rate rise looks likely Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, more than expected, and investors now see roughly a 90% chance the Federal Reserve raises rates on December 9. Safe government bonds pay close to 5%, enough to compete with stocks for investor money, which pulls cash out of the market and makes borrowing dearer for everyone.

The Iran war is still a live risk Thirty seven ships were attacked around the Strait of Hormuz in October, the most since the war began, with about ten more so far in November, and oil bounced back up today. The strait carries about a fifth of the world's oil, so any escalation pushes energy prices and inflation back up and undoes the recent relief.

Bottom line Profits are strong and buyers returned this week, but the market is being pulled between good earnings and stubborn inflation. That argues for owning steady earners with real cash coming in, which is where today's picks lean, rather than chasing the fastest movers.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected return for a stock: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read. A fund carries the same idea under its own label, the ETF score: a standing score anchored on what funds like it have typically returned, not a forecast for that one fund, and its bar is drawn on the same scale as every other pick so the sizes can be compared directly.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
