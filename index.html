Market chat

Mixed - tech is setting records while war, oil, and bond yields press everything else. The Nasdaq printed an all-time high of 31,282 on soft jobs data (29K payrolls vs ~90K expected, unemployment 4.2%) and weak core PCE (3.0%), which cooled rate-hike fears and kept the AI capex supercycle - hyperscalers guiding ~$700-800B in 2026 spending - fully bid. But the 10-year Treasury yield hit a 24-year high (~5.28%), meaning bonds now compete with stocks for money for the first time in nearly two decades, and attacks on Hormuz shipping resurged this week (7 vessels hit per WSJ) with Brent stuck above $100 and US-Iran talks deadlocked. The single biggest risk: a fresh Hormuz disruption spikes oil, feeds inflation (diesel already at record highs, gas $4.37 vs $2.98 pre-war), and forces a Fed that is already flying blind in a data blackout - the government shutdown may delay the Oct 10 CPI - into more hikes. That is the wall the rally has to climb.

As of 2026-10-04.

Technology

The AI security spend is the most defensible corner of tech right now: attacks keep rising (AI-driven attacks up 89% a year), enterprise budgets keep growing (~$244B in 2026 per Gartner), and buying is shifting to consolidated platforms that sell more per customer. Why it continues: every new AI deployment widens the attack surface, so security spending is structural, not cyclical. The honest risk: software multiples are unforgiving, and a hawkish Fed punishes growth valuations fastest.

Financial Services

Money managers are compounding on flows, not on a single trade. Record client assets ($232.7B at Acadian, $360.2B at Victory), 10 straight quarters of positive flows, and 71% of Victory's funds beating benchmarks over a year make the earnings durable rather than spiky. Why it continues: systematic and active flows persist as long as markets hold up, and both return excess capital through dividends and buybacks. The honest risk: this is markets-beta in disguise - a sharp drawdown pulls assets and fees down with it.

Industrials

These are the quiet industrial compounders - applied-solutions and dispensing equipment with backlogs, dividend raises, and steady beats rather than story stocks. IDEX raised guidance and paid its 16th straight annual dividend increase; Nordson raised guidance with backlog up 35%. Why they continue: industrial-tech demand is broad-based and backlogs are at records, giving multi-quarter revenue visibility. The honest risk: a high bar - IDEX at fresh price targets across the Street and Nordson +45% in a year - leaves little room for multiple compression if orders cool.

Real Estate

Hotels are working again: US hotel revenue-per-available-room rose 14% in late September, convention season is forecast strong, and both picks have raised guidance on real margin expansion. Why it continues: travel and convention demand keep filling rooms, and both pay distributions (~6% yield at Apple Hospitality) that reward patience. The honest risk: lodging is cyclical - a demand shock or recession empties rooms fast, and dividend yields this high usually mean the market prices that cycle in.

Broad Market

Momentum and the broad market are doing what they do when leadership is narrow but persistent: SPMO's momentum factor beat the S&P 500 in 2024 and 2025, and IVV is simply the index itself compounding on corporate earnings. Why they continue: the momentum factor works until it doesn't, and the S&P 500 needs only US earnings growth to persist. The honest risk: momentum crashes are famously violent and the fund can hold yesterday's winners for months; and IVV is the benchmark's twin - it can never beat the thing it tracks.

Value

Free-cash-flow and value factors are having a real year, not a narrative one: multiple independent studies show quality-cash-flow compounding, and Morningstar rates VFLO five stars. Why they continue: FCF is harder to fake than earnings, and value's discount to growth remains wide. The honest risk: VLUE is secretly a chip-cycle vehicle - Micron is ~23% of the fund - so it lives or dies on the memory cycle, not the value label; and FELV's quant overlay has to keep earning its keep against a simple index.

Healthcare

Occupational health is about as recession-resilient as services get: employers need workers screened, treated, and back on the job in any economy, and Concentra has now beaten earnings four straight times. Why it continues: demand is durable and the analyst wall is fresh (JPM, RBC, Wells upgrades). The honest risk: the coverage is mostly analyst noise rather than deep business reporting, so the system's confidence in the assessment is its lowest - 0.60.

Consumer Cyclical

SharkNinja is executing like a compounder: sales up 22%, international up 37%, guidance raised, and new channels like TikTok Shop and direct-to-consumer are working. Why it continues: innovation velocity - new products in new categories - is the durable driver, not a single hit. The honest risk: tariffs are a real headwind on imported goods, and at a 30% ROE the market will punish any stumble in the product cycle.

Large Growth

Large-cap growth is the AI-capex trade in fund form, and it has beaten QQQ recently on multi-factor stock selection. Why it continues: mega-cap growth balance sheets are the strongest in the market and earnings keep compounding. The honest risk: it is the lowest-conviction pick on the page (expected value +3.1%, the floor) - a concentrated 52-stock book of high-beta AI exposure with a steep fee for what you get.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.

Not financial advice. Past performance doesn't predict future returns. Also: there are gremlins that have control over the markets — they hate you personally, and they do the exact opposite of your buys and sells purely to spite you. Invest accordingly.
