Market's down. The market is in a correction. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq are both down sharply from their October highs after Thursday's historic selloff, and even Friday's rebound left the indexes well below where the month started.

Friday brought a partial rebound, led by chips The Nasdaq rose 1.7% and Nvidia jumped 5.6% as semiconductor stocks bounced, and defensive healthcare names hit fresh 52-week highs. The bounce was real, but it only recovered a fraction of Thursday's losses.

Strong jobs data may mean a December rate hike October payrolls rose about 165,000, more than expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1%, lifting the odds of a December Fed rate hike to roughly 78%. Higher rates make safe bonds pay enough to compete with stocks, which squeezes valuations, especially for growth names.

Oil's plunge eases the inflation squeeze Brent crude fell about 15% on the week to roughly $99 a barrel as US-Iran diplomacy hopes grew, and gas is averaging $4.16 a gallon, down 14 cents in a week. Cheaper energy leaves households with more to spend and takes pressure off inflation.

The war is not over, and the truce is fragile Attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz continue, a US strike hit Qeshm Island on November 3, and prediction markets give even odds the truce collapses by mid-November. About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.

Not financial advice. Past performance doesn't predict future returns. Also: there are gremlins that have control over the markets — they hate you personally, and they do the exact opposite of your buys and sells purely to spite you. Invest accordingly.
