Market today

Market's down. The broad market has been falling for weeks and is now in a correction, down about 12% from its October record, because the crowded AI trade is unwinding and safe bonds paying close to 5% are pulling money out of stocks.

The market is in a correction The S&P 500 fell 8.6% last week, its worst week since April 2025, and has now dropped seven sessions in a row. The Nasdaq has lost about 10% and is negative for the year, a sharp reversal for an index that set records in October.

The AI trade is unwinding Nvidia reported record earnings on November 17 and still fell about 23% on the week, after its chief executive resigned amid a board investigation and reports of a regulatory accounting inquiry followed. When the stock the whole rally rests on falls on good news, investors sell the crowded trade first and ask questions later.

Everyday businesses are holding up Consumer staples funds recently hit record highs and health care has beaten the broad market this year, while value funds full of cheaper, profitable companies are leading. Walmart beat expectations and raised its forecast on November 19, a sign that household spending on essentials is still healthy.

War and oil keep pressure on prices The US-Iran war is in its ninth month and tankers are still being hit in the Strait of Hormuz, which carries about a fifth of the world's oil. That keeps crude near 2 and inflation stubborn, which keeps the 10-year Treasury yield near 4.92%, and safe bonds paying that much compete directly with stocks for investor money.

The bottom line This is a falling market being led down by its most expensive corner, while profitable, everyday businesses hold up far better. Wednesday's inflation report and the December 8-9 Fed meeting will decide whether Mondays bounce becomes a recovery, so today's picks lean defensive on purpose: steady earners, value funds, and health care over anything that needs the AI trade to come back.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and funds that have already proven themselves, names beating their benchmark across a mix of recent windows: the past year, the past six months, and the past three months. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for funds it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep doing well over the next year, even if the market dips along the way?

The philosophy is simple: guard against big losses, but judge a pick by the year ahead, not by a rough month. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty: every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and fund is screened for strength against its benchmark across those windows, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates bar names that have run too far too fast, fallen too hard, or swing too wildly.

Question the story. Survivors get researched. We read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Last, we put one honest number on each stock: its expected return, what we think it might gain over the next year, the good signs weighed against the warning signs. It is a judgment, not a promise. Its job is comparison, so every name can be ranked on the same terms and only the best odds make the cut. Funds get a grade instead of a forecast: the ETF Score, based mostly on what the fund costs, since cheap funds tend to beat expensive ones, and partly on how calmly it has behaved. A grade describes how a fund is built, not where its price is going.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
