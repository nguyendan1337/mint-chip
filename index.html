Market today

Market's mixed. The broad market is roughly flat over the past month (SPY down just 0.5%) while tech has pulled back (VGT down 5.6%, QQQ down 5.0%) as chip stocks sold off and AI data-center financing tightened; leadership is rotating into healthcare, value, and cash-flow names.

Healthcare is carrying the market Healthcare funds hit a 52-week high this week: steady GLP-1 demand plus new drug-pricing deals that shrank the feared price-control rule to a handful of companies, lifting a big valuation overhang. Today's picks lean in with a healthcare ETF and two medical names.

Cash-flow and value names are in favor Value stocks are up 32% over the past year versus 14% for growth, and the top ETF pick screens for companies throwing off real cash. When money gets expensive, cash in hand beats promises.

Tech is wobbling on AI financing Chip stocks sold off Thursday, and even Nvidia's CEO says capital for new data centers is scarce. The AI build-out still powers tech earnings — but it runs on borrowed money, and if funding tightens, tech estimates come down with it.

Near-5% yields pull money out of stocks The 10-year Treasury pays about 5%, so safe bonds compete directly with stocks for investors' money. The Fed meets Oct 27-28 and the inflation report lands Oct 29, which will set expectations for a December rate move.

The Hormuz conflict keeps oil near $110 The US-Iran war escalated around the Strait of Hormuz, pushing crude to about $112. A fifth of the world's oil flows through it: high oil feeds inflation, inflation pushes rates up, and higher rates weigh on stock prices.

The move: a rotating market, not a roaring one The market isn't climbing or crashing — it's shifting its weight from expensive tech into healthcare, value, and cash-generating names, where today's picks sit. A hawkish Fed surprise next week is the most likely thing to break this fragile balance.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
