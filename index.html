Market today

Market's mixed. The market is bouncing after two shaky weeks, but the bounce is only two days old. The S&P 500 rose 0.7% Monday to 7,676.77 and added roughly another half percent by midday Tuesday, with tech leading, yet oil near $115 and a Federal Reserve decision Wednesday mean the rebound is still unproven.

Chips and AI names are bouncing back Anthropic's filing to go public revived appetite for AI stocks: chip shares rose a third straight day and the Nasdaq climbed about 1% by midday Tuesday, with the S&P 500 up about 0.5%. The rebound is real but narrow, since it follows a bruising few weeks for the same names.

Earnings are doing the heavy lifting for today's picks The strongest names on the list keep beating expectations rather than riding headlines: SEI beat on October 21 with margins widening, SharkNinja has grown double digits for 13 straight quarters, and Nordson posted record results with backlog up 35%. When companies keep raising guidance, their stocks have a reason to hold up.

Oil near $115 keeps the inflation squeeze on October is the worst month yet for attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, and oil flows through the strait are running at roughly 60% of normal, leaving Brent near $115 a barrel. That matters for stocks because expensive fuel pushes up transport costs and prices everywhere, which keeps inflation high and makes it harder for the Fed to cut rates, while safe bonds paying about 5% keep pulling money away from stocks.

A huge week of tests is dead ahead The Fed decides rates on Wednesday and is widely expected to hold, but markets are already pricing at least two more hikes by mid-2027. Thursday brings third-quarter economic growth and the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, and big tech earnings land October 29-30, with Fortinet reporting Wednesday and several of today's names printing Thursday.

The move: stay selective, not fearful Two good days do not erase a shaky month, and two empty ETF slots are the system saying it would rather hold nothing than hold filler. The names that made the list earn it with cash flow, backlog or recurring revenue, and the week ahead will test whether the bounce has legs.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
