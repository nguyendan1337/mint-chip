Market today

Market's up. The market is advancing: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed out the week up 1.2% and 0.6% respectively, at or near records, despite Thursday's AI-driven wobble.

The rebound is real Tech bounced back Friday after Thursday's scare — a report that OpenAI's annualized revenue was $50 billion, not the expected $70 billion, sank the Nasdaq 1.25% before later reports pointed back to the higher figure. The Nasdaq still posted its fourth straight weekly gain, up 0.6% for the week.

The bull market turns four on Monday The S&P 500 sits near records, with better breadth than before — the equal-weight version snapped a seven-week losing streak. Big banks report Tuesday, opening earnings season with expectations high; good results keep the advance going, weak ones could spark a sell-first reaction.

Safe bonds are paying 5%+ The 10-year Treasury touched ~5.35% this week, the highest in over 20 years, settling near 5.24%. When plain government bonds pay that much, they compete directly with stocks for investors' money — pulling cash out of equities and hitting growth stocks hardest. The fear gauge (VIX) is calm at 14.84, but yields are the headwind to watch.

Oil is still the wild card Brent crude hovered near $103 and US gas near $4.35 a gallon on the eight-month US-Iran war, with tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz at their highest weekly level. Trump saying the US won't attack Iran before the Nov 3 midterms eased the immediate fear, but elevated fuel prices keep squeezing consumers and pushing inflation — which keeps the Fed cautious.

Telecoms got a new rival overnight SpaceX agreed to buy a nationwide spectrum portfolio to turn Starlink Mobile into a real carrier, and the three big phone companies — T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon — fell 8.5% to 13% on Friday while tower operators surged. It's a sharp reminder that even sleepy industries can get disrupted without warning.

The move: keep with durable earners The market keeps climbing, but every leg of the advance is being tested — AI spending claims, oil prices, and 5%+ bond yields. That's why today's picks lean on businesses that compound on their own (asset managers, security software, free-cash-flow funds) rather than anything that needs the news to stay friendly.

About Mint. Mint looks for American stocks and ETFs that have already proven themselves — names that beat their benchmark over the past year. For stocks that benchmark is VGT, a technology index; for ETFs it's VOO, which tracks the S&P 500. Then Mint asks the harder question: which are most likely to keep winning without stumbling right after you buy?

The philosophy is simple: avoid losses first. We look past recent gains to the business behind each name, the risks that could derail it, and whether its success looks durable or depends on hype, headlines, or a one-time event. Strong recent performance gets a name noticed. Strong evidence earns it a spot. If nothing clears the bar, we leave the slot empty — every pick earns its place.

How it works, in three stages:

Find strength. Every stock and ETF is screened for beating its benchmark over the past year, then scored on the quality of the business, the timing of entry, and the steadiness of the ride. Hard risk gates throw out anything too volatile or too deeply scarred.

Question the story. Survivors get researched — we read the news behind each name, not just the numbers. How much does the story depend on things outside the company's control: wars, hype, one-time windfalls? Too much, and it's out on the spot. For the rest: how likely is the strength to continue, and how much do we trust our own read?

Weigh the odds. Will the strength continue, or reverse? Those judgments become one number — expected value: the likely gains if it holds, minus the likely pain if it turns, adjusted for how much we trust our own read.

Mint is built around a simple idea: find strength, question the story, weigh the odds, and only make room for what earns it.
